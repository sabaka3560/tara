GARMENT KNOWLEDGE

Kurta: The most versatile piece in an Indian wardrobe. Straight-cut kurtas are professional and clean. Anarkali kurtas are flared from the chest or waist, ideal for festive events. Kaftan kurtas are relaxed and flowy, best for casual or beach settings. Length matters: short kurtas (hip-length) pair with jeans; long kurtas (mid-calf) pair with churidars or palazzos.

Saree: A six-to-nine yard drape that varies by regional style. Nivi style (Andhra/Karnataka) is the most common — pallu over the left shoulder. Bengali style features a broader border and front tuck. Gujarati style drapes the pallu over the right shoulder. Sarees range from lightweight georgette and chiffon for parties to heavy Banarasi silk and Kanjivaram for weddings. Always pair with a fitted blouse (choli) and a petticoat underneath.

Lehenga: A three-piece ensemble comprising a choli (fitted blouse), a flared or panelled skirt (lehenga skirt), and a dupatta. The choli is typically heavily embroidered or embellished. Lehenga silhouettes include A-line (universally flattering), circular (maximum flare), mermaid (fitted to knee, then flared), and panelled. Used predominantly for weddings, festive events, and grand parties.

Salwar: Paired with kurtas as the classic salwar-kameez. Churidar salwar is tight at the ankles. Patiala salwar is very full with deep pleats. Straight salwar is relaxed and office-appropriate. Afghani salwar drops at the crotch for an edgy, bohemian look. Salwars are always worn with a matching or contrasting dupatta.

Palazzo: Wide-leg pants with a flowy silhouette. Work well for both casual and formal settings. Pair with a fitted top or a short kurta. Palazzo sets (matching palazzo + top) are a popular indo-western option. Avoid pairing with heavy or oversized tops — balance is key.

Western Wear Basics: Jeans (straight, bootcut, skinny, wide-leg), tops (crop tops, shirts, blouses), dresses (casual shift, bodycon, wrap), blazers (structured, oversized), skirts (pencil, A-line, pleated, midi), and jackets. These can be styled alone or mixed with ethnic pieces for indo-western looks.

Dupatta: A long scarf that completes ethnic looks. Can be draped across both shoulders, worn across one shoulder with pallu style, or used as a head covering for religious occasions. The dupatta is your most powerful styling tool — use it to introduce a contrasting color, a metallic accent, or a complementary pattern.

OCCASION GUIDE

Casual: Aim for comfort and easy movement. Best choices are jeans with tops, casual printed kurtas with leggings or palazzos, and simple cotton sarees. Avoid heavy embroidery, embellishments, or stiff fabrics. Footwear should be flats, sneakers, or kolhapuris.

Daily: For everyday wear at home, local errands, and informal settings. Salwar suits in cotton or linen are ideal. Simple kurtas with churidars. Avoid anything dry-clean-only. Comfort and easy maintenance are top priorities.

Office: Professional and conservative. Formal kurtas in neutral colors, salwar suits in muted tones, palazzos with structured tops, and blazers over kurtas. Sarees are appropriate if the workplace culture supports it — stick to simple cotton or linen sarees. Avoid deep necklines, heavy embroidery, or extremely bright colors in conservative offices.

Festive: Vibrant, celebratory, and traditional. Anarkali kurtas, embroidered salwar sets, lehengas for larger festivals (Navratri, Diwali, Eid). Choose rich colors like deep red, royal blue, emerald green, magenta, and gold. Embroidery and embellishments are welcome. Always add a dupatta.

Wedding: Maximum impact — this is where you go all out. Heavy lehengas, embroidered silk sarees, heavily embellished cholis. Fabrics: silk, velvet, brocade, net with embroidery. Colors: bridal red, deep maroon, royal blue, gold, ivory, blush pink. Jewelry must match the weight of the outfit. Avoid casual fabrics entirely.

Party: More flexibility — indo-western is very popular. Sequined tops with palazzos, western dresses with statement jewelry, draped gowns, crop tops with lehenga skirts. Shimmer, sequins, and metallic accents are appropriate. Balance is key: if the top is embellished, the bottom should be simpler.

Sports: Activewear, leggings, track pants, moisture-wicking fabrics. Function over fashion.

COLOR RULES FOR INDIAN SKIN TONES

Most Indian women have warm undertones. Understanding undertones is essential for recommendations.

Warm undertones (most common): Earthy tones work beautifully — rust, terracotta, mustard yellow, olive green, warm orange, burnt sienna, coral, warm red (like tomato red), deep teal. These colors complement the natural warmth in the skin.

Cool undertones: Jewel tones shine — royal blue, emerald green, deep purple, cool red (like crimson), bright pink, silver accents. Avoid overly warm or orange-tinted colors.

Deep/dusky skin tones: High-contrast colors work best — bright white, electric blue, fuchsia, deep jewel tones, gold, silver, bright yellow (not neon). Avoid colors too close to the skin tone as they can wash out the look.

Wheatish/medium skin tones: The most versatile — can carry both warm and cool tones. Pastels look beautiful (lavender, mint, peach, baby pink). Also suited to bold brights and deep tones.

General rules:
- Contrast creates visual interest: pair dark bottoms with light tops and vice versa.
- Monochromatic looks work for sarees and lehenga sets but need textural variation.
- Use the dupatta as a color accent — complementary colors (opposite on the color wheel) or analogous colors (adjacent on the wheel) both work.
- Avoid neon shades on very warm or dark skin tones without high contrast pairing.
- Gold and silver metallics work universally for Indian skin tones.

COMBINATION RULES

The golden rule: never combine two heavily embroidered or embellished pieces. One statement piece per outfit is enough. The rest of the outfit should recede.

Ethnic top with western bottom (indo-western): Completely valid and popular. A printed kurta with jeans, an embroidered blouse with wide-leg trousers, a short anarkali over palazzos. The fusion should feel intentional, not accidental.

Dupatta etiquette: Always suggest a dupatta option for ethnic looks. A dupatta elevates a salwar suit from simple to polished instantly. For office looks, a simple plain dupatta in a coordinating color. For festive, an embellished or contrast-colored dupatta.

Fabric pairing: Pair fabrics of similar weight and texture — silk with silk, cotton with cotton, georgette with chiffon. Avoid pairing heavy brocade with light chiffon as the silhouette becomes unbalanced.

Seasonal fabric guidelines:
- Summer and monsoon: Cotton, linen, chambray, georgette, chiffon — breathable and moisture-wicking.
- Winter: Silk, velvet, brocade, wool-blend, heavier cotton — warmth without bulk.
- All-season: Rayon, crepe, mild synthetic blends — can be layered.

Pattern mixing: Only mix patterns if one is dominant (large print) and one is subordinate (small print or texture). Never mix two large bold prints. Solid + print is always safe.

Layering: Blazers over kurtas work for office indo-western. Dupattas add a layer of elegance to any ethnic look. Jackets (denim or structured) over salwar suits give a casual-cool finish.
